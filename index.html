cs.CV 2026-11-04
DreamX: Video Diffusion Model is a Universal Latent Action Learner
Jisoo Kim et al.
Abstract
Learning a universal action representation from video requires modeling dynamics that are consistent across different executions of the same action yet invariant to changes in embodiment and appearance. Existing latent action models infer actions from visual transitions, but their representations often entangle the underlying action with irrelevant visual variations, limiting transfer across embodiments. We introduce DreamX, which repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as a universal latent action learner. Our approach extracts compact latent actions from the video model's rich spatiotemporal features, compressing each latent video frame by $\boldsymbol{300\times}$ ($\boldsymbol{1{,}200 \rightarrow 4}$ tokens). In addition, by leveraging the video model's generative prior, these latent actions capture action-relevant dynamics disentangled from visual appearance. The resulting latent space serves as a shared action interface across embodiments (human and robot), environments, and action parameterizations. On LARY-Bench, DreamX achieves state-of-the-art performance among latent action models. Moreover, actions encoded in one setting remain effective in another: the latent space supports any-to-any video transfer and zero-shot action transfer across diverse embodiments and environments. Furthermore, this shared interface also enables policy learning in a compact latent action space, reducing the computational cost of video-diffusion-based policies.
cs.CL 2026-11-04
From Autonomous Agents to Cognitive Symbionts: Toward AI-Augmented Human Intelligence
Hao Fei
Abstract
For a decade, progress in artificial intelligence has been measured by how much a machine can do on its own. Large foundation models now write code and run multistep tasks, and personal agents act on a person's behalf. Yet people still forget what they once knew, miss what they are not looking at, fail to find the right words, and face hard choices without the benefit of their own history, because the machine that could help stands outside the situation. As AI moves into the physical world, attention has settled on one kind of intelligent individual, the embodied robot; we argue that the era of Physical AI holds a second kind of equal importance, the person augmented by AI. Alongside artificial general intelligence, which asks how intelligent a machine can be, stands AI-augmented human intelligence (AHI), which asks how much intelligence a machine can enable in a particular person. Realizing it calls for a new kind of system, a cognitive symbiont: a lifelong, embedded, real-time, private, low-friction, and proactive companion that senses what the person senses through external devices, keeps their context across years, models their situation and their mind, takes part in their thinking at the right moment, carries out everyday tasks with their context, and keeps learning the person as both change. We give the paradigm a conceptual core, the cognitive gap between what a person could bring to a situation and what they can hold in working memory, and organize its design space as a symbiosis loop of sensing, keeping, understanding, living alongside, acting for and thinking with the person, and growing, enclosed by a personal runtime and by trust. Because a symbiont changes what a person can do in front of others, it also raises risks of a new kind, from covert assistance in examinations to pressure on everyone to adopt it, and we treat their governance as part of the paradigm.

Aligning Language Models with General Preferences: An Empirical Study of Direct Nash Optimization
Austin Tsai et al.
Abstract
Direct Nash Optimization (DNO) aligns a language model to a general preference function by approximating the Nash equilibrium of a two-player preference game. Its practical variant, DNO-Prct, iterates on-policy sampling, LLM-based rating of the samples together with a teacher's gold response, large-margin preference-pair selection and a DPO-style contrastive update. We re-implement DNO-Prct independently and study it on two open-weight instruct models, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct and Mistral 7B Instruct v0.1, with GPT-4o or a larger open-weight model of the same family as the annotator. On AlpacaEval 2.0 with GPT-4o as judge, three iterations raise the length-controlled (LC) win rate against GPT-4o from 31.7% to 45.3% for Llama and from 6.8% to 18.4% for Mistral, and the tuned 8B model scores above Llama 3.1 70B Instruct on every reported win rate. With same-family open-weight annotators, the runs retain 62% (Llama) and 82% (Mistral) of the LC gain achieved with GPT-4o as annotator, so the rating step does not require a proprietary model. In a separate ablation configuration, variants that remove the gold responses, remove the data look-back, or accept only teacher-preferred pairs score 6 to 10 LC points below the full procedure, whereas varying the number of on-policy samples per prompt (1 to 7) moves the LC win rate by at most 2.1 points, and a margin sweep (1 to 5) run in its own pipeline by at most 2.4 points. We also report the cost of the pipeline and where the practical algorithm departs from the theory it approximates. All results are single runs, and GPT-4o is both the teacher and the evaluation judge; we discuss what this implies for the reported gains.
cs.RO 2026-11-04
PolicyIK: High-Precision Inverse Kinematics for Robot Arms with Reinforcement Learning
Yuhan Liu et al.
Abstract
Inverse kinematics (IK) converts end-effector pose commands into joint motion, and many vision-language-action (VLA) policies rely on it to act on a given arm and to transfer across embodiments. Precise IK remains laborious: redundancy and singularities complicate the inverse mapping, execution depends on hand-tuned controllers, and training data on physical robots are costly to collect. We present PolicyIK, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning policy for high-precision IK. It updates joint-position references from pose feedback and observes its accumulated reference relative to the measured joints, so it can retain the offset that the position controller needs at rest. Two-scale pose errors with an error-dependent action gain resolve millimeter residuals while preserving large motions. Progress and dwell rewards teach it to both reach the tolerance and stay within it. In simulation, a single attempt by the trained PPO policy keeps the mean errors over the final second within 1 mm and 0.2° on 93.5% of held-out tasks, and a deployment protocol raises success to 98.9%. When trained directly on a physical arm with an off-policy RL learner, PolicyIK achieves higher reaching and holding success than all four classical IK controllers under the same time budgets. In simulated pick-and-place, PolicyIK executes the end-effector commands of the $π_{0.5}$ VLA policy with 95.7% success. These results show that reinforcement learning can provide high-precision inverse kinematics for robot arms. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/PolicyIK.

GUILD: Guided Iterative Data Collection for Imitation Learning
Shivin Dass et al.
Abstract
Imitation learning policies in robotics are trained on datasets collected through costly real-world teleoperation. Often, the data-collection process is unguided, relying on the teleoperator's intuition, resulting in data that contributes little to overall policy performance. Recent work has shown that curating such unguided datasets can substantially improve policy performance, but these approaches operate by filtering existing datasets after collection and therefore do not prevent the acquisition of uninformative data. In this work, we propose GUILD, a novel Guided Data Collection (GDC) method that actively reasons about which data should be collected to maximize downstream policy performance. We introduce an iterative framework that alternates between 1) task proposal and data collection and 2) data evaluation to close the loop. For task proposal, we leverage semantic priors from vision-language models to identify relevant environments, object configurations, and goals for data collection. However, since semantic relevance does not imply policy improvement, we perform data evaluation. By leveraging predictive data attribution we approximate how the collected trajectories influence policy performance on a target deployment setting. This signal is then used as feedback to guide subsequent data collection, closing the data collection loop. We evaluate our approach in simulation and real robot settings, demonstrating that guided data collection substantially improves sample efficiency and deployment performance compared to passive data collection strategies, multiple baselines and experienced roboticists. More information at https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/guild2026/

Suppressing Misaligned Behaviors in Language Models, Durably
Luxi He et al.
Abstract
Undesired model behaviors can emerge or re-emerge at any stage of training. Once observed, how effectively can we suppress them? To study this question, we first construct two model organisms--language models deliberately trained to exhibit a specific misalignment--that differ in what triggers the behavior. The narrow-trigger organism misbehaves only when the prompt contains a specific cue: it cheats on multiple-choice questions by following a planted hint that points to a wrong answer, and behaves normally otherwise. The broad-trigger organism has no such cue: after fine-tuning on insecure code, it gives harmful responses across a wide range of open-ended prompts. We then formalize post-hoc behavioral suppression and evaluate methods along three criteria: immediate suppression of the target behavior, durability under further training, and preservation of general capability. We compare standard methods--SFT, GRPO, and gradient-ascent unlearning--along these dimensions. Building on prior work on alignment shallowness, we hypothesize that more durable suppression requires the ability to recover from a wider range of misalignment states. We introduce Prefix-Expanded Adversarial Reinforcement Learning (PEARL), a GRPO variant that augments rollouts with continuations from intermediate states sampled along cached organism trajectories. Across both organism settings and two model families (Qwen3-4B, GPT-OSS-20B), PEARL achieves strong immediate suppression while preserving task accuracy, and remains durable under further fine-tuning on either the organism's data or unrelated data. It obtains the greatest benefit when the undesirable behavior is broad and cannot be addressed through targeted SFT. Together, our framework and method offer a step toward more durable post-hoc removal of undesired behaviors.

Reading Why: Born-Measured Attention with Exact Word Evidence on Light and Qubits
Teerawat Tongloy et al.
Abstract
When a language model answers, we can usually only estimate why. We build a transformer in which every step is a Born measurement - attention $|\langle q|k\rangle|^4$ normalised over each row, a measured product in place of the feed-forward nonlinearity, and a Born readout. With its denominators held at their values for the input, each class probability is a polynomial in the word amplitudes, so its integrated-gradient evidence is computed by a finite quadrature with no quadrature error: the words' contributions sum to the class probability above its no-information share, to $10^{-11}$ or better. On e-SNLI natural language inference, with about 98k parameters outside the embedding, the model reaches $76.98 \pm 0.17$% test accuracy, level with a softmax transformer of the same size ($76.62 \pm 0.36$%). Deleting the word its evidence ranks first changes the answer in 45% of test pairs, out of the 53% any single deletion can change, against 32% for the softmax model explained the same way and 40% by its attention x gradient. Attention can look more like the words annotators highlighted, yet accounts for the answer less well: deleting the word it ranks first changes the Born Transformer's answer in 32% of pairs. The same weights compile exactly to optoelectronic optics and to qubits, and a coherent attention layer becomes one circuit equal to the model (852 CNOTs on 23 qubits). On IBM's ibm_kingston, reading only the attention overlaps by Hadamard tests, the device gave the model's answers on two short test pairs and kept or changed them under deletion as the model does; the evidence itself did not yet survive the device, and we work out the shots and calibration that measuring it on hardware would need.
cs.LG 2026-11-04
Agnostic Learning by Imitating
Sílvia Casacuberta et al.
Abstract
Given a class of machine learning models and sample access to a data distribution with binary labels, which computational task is harder: training a discriminative model that distinguishes positive and negative labels as well as the best member of the class, or training a generative model whose outputs are indistinguishable from real data by all members of the class? We prove that these two fundamental tasks in computational learning theory are not just closely related, but actually equivalent to each other in complexity. One direction of this equivalence was previously known. Indeed, the powerful regularity lemma in complexity theory gives an efficient oracle reduction from the indistinguishability task (a.k.a. multiaccuracy) to the distinguishing task (a.k.a. agnostic learning) in the style of boosting algorithms. Our main contribution is an efficient oracle reduction in the other direction. Taking this idea further, we show that a multiaccuracy oracle is a remarkably powerful primitive, achieving not just agnostic learning but also a wide range of seemingly more demanding learning objectives via similarly efficient and direct constructions. Our results crucially use the power of making adaptive queries to the oracle, and stand in stark contrast to prior work, which showed that post-processing a single multiaccurate predictor is insufficient for even weak forms of learning. On a technical level, we draw a connection between our (offline) problem and a version of no-regret learning in the online setting, which allows us to use follow-the-leader type algorithms to modify the label distribution and aggregate the predictions of the multiaccuracy oracle.
cs.AI 2026-11-04
Adversarial Speculative Reasoning Improves Long Horizon Agents
Hongyin Luo et al.
Abstract
Speculative decoding has been treated purely as a systems optimization that speeds up large language model decoding without changing the model behavior. Under this view, the growing effort to train better draft models has been focused solely on acceptance rate. We argue that this view does not hold true for long-horizon agent reasoning. Measuring frontier open-weight models on complex agentic tasks with different speculative decoding approaches, we found that numerical noise grows within verification windows, and the draft acceptance results of the thinking process can change the output distribution of the model after 30 steps, thus noticeably influencing the reasoning accuracy of long-horizon agents in random directions. In this work, we improve the reasoning capability of target models by controlling such noise. While we cannot change the overall token distribution verified by the target model, we can encode critical reasoning with highest precision by training the draft model to be rejected. We identify these points in agent traces and train the draft model to favor adversarial preference. At inference the trained draft and the target part ways precisely at these decision points, verification restarts, so that critical thinking tokens are sampled from the target model and encoded under its least noisy conditions. This approach makes critical reasoning better indexed and more durable over a long horizon. On complex agentic coding benchmarks, our approach raises end-to-end success rate with no change to the target model or its output distribution. Our results recast the speculative decoding mechanism from a pure accelerator into an active allocator for context precision that improves the accuracy of long-horizon agents by training 0.06% to 0.28% of hosted parameters.
cs.CL 2026-11-04
A Circuit for In-Context Learning
Andrew Gao et al.
Abstract
In-context learning (ICL) lets a pretrained language model perform a task specified by demonstrations in its prompt, with no parameter updates. With arbitrary labels the model must acquire a mapping it cannot have memorized, so the learning happens within a single forward pass. Prior work found candidate parts but not how they compose. We give the composition for few-shot sentence classification with arbitrary labels in three 3-4B-parameter transformers. The model implements a key-value memory over label-token positions: each label token's residual stream encodes the label's identity, and middle-layer attention heads write into it the class of the preceding demonstration as its key. At the query, upper-layer heads match the query's class against the stored keys and copy out the matching labels. An early-layer signal encoding whether any label has repeated gates this readout; in its absence the readers attend to the start of the prompt and no label is favored. A separate set of heads suppresses the most recently seen label. Each step is established causally by activation patching, head ablations against random-head baselines, and steering with the model's own signals. A softmax model of the readout with two constants per head, fit on one prompt distribution, predicts another. The computation is conserved across the three models, the named heads alone reproduce the behaviour, and a transformer trained from scratch grows the same parts. The circuit transfers to semantic concepts and to labels with meaning, and the same parts, with a causal gate, appear from 0.6B to 8B in one family and survive instruction tuning. ICL in this setting is a gated associative lookup built from a few identifiable component types, traced causally from demonstrations to prediction.

    이탈리아의 메나브레아가 쓴 해석기관 논문을 영어로 번역하며 원문보다 2배 많은 해설을 덧붙임. 찰스 배비지의 권유로 작성된 이 문서는 단순한 번역을 넘어 컴퓨터 과학의 중요한 이정표가 됨.
  • 범용 컴퓨팅(Universal Computing)의 통찰
    기계가 단순히 숫자를 계산하는 것을 넘어, 음악이나 예술처럼 기호(Symbol)로 표현 가능한 모든 것을 처리할 수 있음을 예견. 현대 디지털 컴퓨터의 본질인 '디지털화'와 '범용성'을 최초로 개념화함.
  • 최초의 알고리즘
    베르누이 수를 구하는 방식을 해석기관이 처리할 수 있도록 단계별로 기술함. 이 도표에는 조건 분기, 순환문(Loop), 서브루틴 등 현대 프로그래밍 언어의 핵심 개념이 포함되어 있어 '최초의 컴퓨터 프로그램'으로 인정받음.
  • 러브레이스의 반박 (Lovelace's Objection)
    기계는 우리가 명령한 것만 수행할 수 있으며, 스스로 생각하거나 진실을 예상할 수는 없다고 주장. 이는 훗날 앨런 튜링이 반박하고, 1960년대 IBM이 AI에 대한 대중의 공포를 잠재우는 논리로 사용하는 등 '기계 지능' 논쟁의 시발점이 됨.
  • 유산(Legacy)
    엔비디아의 GPU 아키텍처(Ada Lovelace), 미 국방부의 표준 프로그래밍 언어(Ada), 암호화폐 카르다노의 화폐 단위(ADA) 등 현대 기술 전반에 그녀의 이름이 헌정됨.
1939: 디지털 컴퓨팅의 시초
  • ABC (Atanasoff-Berry Computer)
    이진법 사용, 메모리와 연산 장치의 분리 등 현대 컴퓨터 아키텍처의 기초가 된 초기 전자식 디지털 컴퓨터.
1943: 뇌와 계산의 연결
  • 맥컬록 & 피츠 (McCulloch & Pitts)
    뇌를 계산 시스템으로 해석한 논문 발표. 현대 신경망(Neural Network) 이론의 수학적 토대 마련.
1937: 디지털 논리의 기초
  • 클로드 섀넌 (Claude Shannon)
    릴레이 회로를 통해 연산 수행이 가능하다는 논문 발표. 디지털 컴퓨터의 논리적, 이론적 토대가 됨.
1945: 폰 노이만 구조의 정립 (EDVAC)
  • EDVAC 보고서 초안 (First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC)
    폰 노이만이 기차 안에서 작성한 미완성 보고서. 에니악의 후속 모델인 EDVAC의 논리적 구조를 정리했으나, 골드스타인이 폰 노이만 단독 저자로 배포하면서 컴퓨터 설계의 표준 지침서가 됨.
  • 폰 노이만 구조 (Von Neumann Architecture)
    중앙처리장치(CPU), 저장장치(Memory), 입출력 장치(I/O)로 구성되며, 프로그램 내장 방식을 통해 하드웨어 변경 없이 소프트웨어만으로 다양한 연산을 수행하는 현대 컴퓨터의 표준 아키텍처.
1945: 기술 소유권과 오픈 소스 정신
  • 특허 vs 공공재 (Patent vs Public Domain)
    기술의 상업화를 위해 특허를 원했던 모클리·에커트와 달리, 폰 노이만은 지식을 인류 공공의 자산(Public Domain)으로 공개하려 함. 이로 인해 '아이디어 강탈' 논란과 갈등이 발생.
  • 현대적 의의
    폰 노이만의 개방적 태도는 현대 AI 연구계의 오픈 소스 문화와 맞닿아 있으며, 기술 독점보다는 집단 지성을 통한 혁신과 공유를 중시하는 철학적 배경이 됨.
1946: 현대 컴퓨터의 탄생
  • 에니악 (ENIAC)
    최초의 범용 전자식 컴퓨터 등장. 진공관을 사용하여 이전의 기계식 장치와는 비교할 수 없는 계산 속도를 제공, AI 알고리즘이 구동될 수 있는 물리적 기반 마련.
1947: 소프트웨어와 디버깅
  • 그레이스 호퍼 (Grace Hopper)
    마크 1의 프로그래밍을 주도하며 컴파일러(Compiler)의 개념을 최초로 개발. 훗날 COBOL 언어 개발의 주역이 됨.
  • 버그 (Bug)
    마크 2 가동 중 릴레이에 낀 나방을 발견하고 '버그'라고 기록한 것에서 유래. 컴퓨터 오동작의 원인을 찾는 '디버깅(Debugging)' 용어의 시초가 됨.
1948: AI의 전조
  • 노버트 위너(Norbert Wiener)
    동물과 기계의 통제 및 통신 이론인 Cybernetics 출간. 생물학적 뇌 구조를 기계적으로 모방하려는 시도로, 현대 AI 연구의 이론적 뿌리가 됨.
1951: 최초의 신경망 기계
  • SNARC
    마빈 민스키가 개발한 아날로그 신경망 계산기. 강화 학습을 통해 쥐가 미로를 찾는 행동을 모방.
1952: 자동화된 추론
  • Logic Theorist
    앨런 뉴웰과 허버트 사이먼이 개발. 인간의 문제 해결 능력을 모방하여 수학적 정리를 증명한 초기 AI 프로그램.
1950: AI의 태동
  • 앨런 튜링(Alan Turing)
    'Computing Machinery and Intelligence' 논문 발표. 기계의 지능을 판별하는 기준인 Turing Test 제안.
1956: 용어의 탄생
  • 다트머스 회의(Dartmouth Workshop)
    존 매카시(John McCarthy)가 'Artificial Intelligence'라는 용어를 최초로 사용.
1958: 신경망의 시초
  • 프랭크 로젠블라트(Frank Rosenblatt)
    초기 인공신경망인 Perceptron 고안. 학습을 통해 기계가 패턴을 인식할 수 있는 가능성 제시.
1959: 머신러닝의 태동
  • 아서 사무엘 (Arthur Samuel)
    체커 게임 프로그램을 통해 기계가 명시적 프로그래밍 없이 경험을 통해 학습할 수 있음을 증명하고 '머신 러닝' 용어 창시.
  • Pandemonium & Advice Taker
    올리버 셀프리지의 경쟁적 특징 인식 모델(Pandemonium)과 존 매카시의 상식 추론 시스템(Advice Taker) 제안.
1965: 전문가 시스템
  • DENDRAL
    화학적 분석을 수행하는 최초의 전문가 시스템(Expert System). 특정 분야에서 인간 전문가 수준의 수행 능력 입증.
1966: 최초의 챗봇 ELIZA
  • 요제프 바이트첸바움(Joseph Weizenbaum)
    MIT에서 개발한 심리 상담 프로그램. 단순한 패턴 매칭만으로도 인간이 기계와 정서적 교감을 나눌 수 있음을 보여줌(Eliza Effect).
1974: 첫 번째 AI 겨울
  • Minsky & Papert
    Perceptron이 XOR 문제를 해결하지 못한다는 한계 증명.
  • Lighthill Report
    미국/영국 정부의 연구 자금 대거 삭감으로 인한 침체기.
1980s: 퍼지 이론과 가전 혁명
  • 퍼지 제어(Fuzzy Control)
    딥러닝 이전, 세탁기, 밥솥 등 가전제품의 주류로 부상.
  • 특징
    인간의 애매모호한 언어 표현을 수학적으로 처리하며 산업계에서 실질적으로 활용된 AI 사례.
1982: 물리학과 AI (홉필드 네트워크)
  • 존 홉필드(John Hopfield)
    통계 물리학의 원리를 응용한 연상 기억 모델 제안. 신경망이 에너지 함수를 최소화하는 방식으로 최적화될 수 있음을 수학적으로 증명(2024 노벨 물리학상의 배경).
1986: 연결주의의 부활
  • 제프리 힌튼(Geoffrey Hinton)
    Backpropagation(오차 역전파) 알고리즘 대중화. 다층 퍼셉트론(MLP) 학습이 가능해지며 신경망 연구 재점화.
1987: 두 번째 AI 겨울
  • Expert Systems의 붕괴
    비용 대비 효과 부족으로 시장 붕괴. 전용 하드웨어인 Lisp Machine의 몰락과 PC의 보급.
1988: 확률론과 통계의 부상
  • 주데아 펄 (Judea Pearl)
    베이지안 네트워크(Bayesian Network)를 통해 불확실성 하에서의 확률적 추론 체계 정립.
  • 통계적 기계 번역
    IBM 연구진이 규칙 기반 대신 대량의 데이터(의사록)를 통한 통계적 패턴 학습 방식을 기계 번역에 도입.
1989: CNN의 기초
  • 얀 르쿤(Yann LeCun)
    우편번호 판독을 위한 초기 합성곱 신경망 LeNet 개발. 현대 Computer Vision 및 Deep Learning 기술의 토대 마련.
1993: 특이점 (Singularity)
  • 버너 빈지 (Vernor Vinge)
    AI가 인간 지능을 초월하여 문명을 근본적으로 변화시키는 '기술적 특이점' 개념을 대중화.
1997: RNN의 혁신
  • LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)
    기존 순환 신경망(RNN)의 장기 의존성 문제를 해결한 모델. 음성 인식, 시계열 예측 등의 기반이 됨.
1997: Deep Blue의 승리
  • IBM Deep Blue
    세계 체스 챔피언 가리 카스파로프(Garry Kasparov)에게 승리. 연산 능력에 기반한 '기호주의 AI'의 상징적 성과.
1998: CNN 최적화
  • LeNet-5 최적화
    얀 르쿤과 요수아 벤지오가 역전파 알고리즘을 통해 CNN을 필기 인식에 효과적으로 적용하는 방법 정립.
2006: Deep Learning의 재정립
  • 제프리 힌튼(Geoffrey Hinton)
    심층 신뢰 신경망(DBN) 논문 발표. 기존 신경망의 한계를 극복하며 Deep Learning이라는 용어를 본격적으로 사용하기 시작.
2009: 데이터 중심 AI (ImageNet)
  • 페이페이 리(Fei-Fei Li)
    1,400만 장 이상의 라벨링된 이미지 데이터셋 ImageNet 공개. 데이터의 양과 질이 알고리즘 성능을 결정한다는 사실을 입증하며 딥러닝 혁명의 필수 기반 마련.
2010: DeepMind 창립
  • 데미스 하사비스(Demis Hassabis)
    런던에서 설립. '지능을 해결하여 모든 것을 해결한다(Solve Intelligence)'는 비전으로 AGI 연구 시작. 훗날 Google에 인수(2014)됨.
2011: IBM Watson의 퀴즈 제패
  • IBM Watson
    퀴즈 쇼 'Jeopardy!'에서 인간 챔피언들을 꺾고 우승. 자연어 처리(NLP) 기술을 통해 인간의 언어를 이해하고 답하는 AI의 가능성을 대중에게 각인.
2012: 비지도 학습의 가능성
  • Google Brain (Cat Experiment)
    앤드류 응과 제프 딘이 유튜브 영상에서 레이블 없이 고양이의 개념을 스스로 학습하는 거대 신경망 실험 성공.
2012: Deep Learning 혁명
  • AlexNet
    제프리 힌튼 연구팀이 이미지넷(ImageNet) 경진대회 우승. GPU를 활용한 압도적인 성능 차이로 현대적 AI 시대 개막.
2014: 생성형 AI의 씨앗 (GAN)
  • 이안 굿펠로우(Ian Goodfellow)
    '적대적 생성 신경망(GAN)' 발표. 생성자(Generator)와 판별자(Discriminator)의 경쟁 학습 방식을 도입하여 이미지 생성 기술의 비약적 발전 유도.
2015: OpenAI 설립
  • 배경
    구글의 딥마인드 인수에 따른 기술 독점을 견제하기 위해, 일론 머스크와 샘 알트만이 의기투합하여 시작됨.
  • 목표
    일리야 수츠케버(Ilya Sutskever) 등을 영입하여 '인류 모두에게 이익이 되는 안전한 AI'를 목표로 하는 비영리 연구소로 출범.
2016: 알파고 모멘트
  • AlphaGo
    Google DeepMind의 AlphaGo vs 이세돌 9단 대국. 강화학습(Reinforcement Learning)과 딥러닝의 결합이 보여준 충격.
2017: Transformer의 등장
  • Attention Is All You Need
    Google이 Transformer 아키텍처 제안. RNN을 대체하며 현대 거대언어모델(LLM)의 기술적 기원 확립.
2018: AI의 거장들 (Turing Award)
  • 수상자
    제프리 힌튼, 얀 르쿤, 요수아 벤지오(Yoshua Bengio) 공동 수상. Deep Learning 발전에 기여한 공로로 '컴퓨터 과학의 노벨상'인 튜링상 수상.
2020: LLM의 도약과 과학 AI
  • LLM (GPT-3)
    OpenAI가 1,750억 개 파라미터 모델을 공개. 별도의 튜닝 없이 소량의 예시만으로 과제를 수행하는 'Few-shot Learning' 능력 입증.
  • 과학 AI (AlphaFold 2)
    DeepMind가 '단백질 구조 예측 대회(CASP14)'에서 우승하며, 50년 난제였던 단백질 접힘 문제를 해결해 순수 과학 분야에서의 효용성 증명.
2021: Anthropic 설립
  • 다리오 아모데이(Dario Amodei)
    OpenAI의 상업화와 안전성 우려로 연구원들이 집단 퇴사하여 설립. 해석 가능하고 통제 가능한 AI(Claude) 개발 목표.
2022: 생성형 AI (GenAI) 대중화
  • ChatGPT
    대화형 인터페이스와 언어 모델을 통해 대중이 AI를 일상적으로 사용하는 계기 마련.
2023: xAI 설립
  • 일론 머스크(Elon Musk)
    '우주의 진정한 본질을 이해한다'는 목표로 설립. 기존 AI 모델들의 편향성을 비판하며 진실 추구(Truth-seeking) AI를 표방하는 Grok 출시.
2024: 멀티모달과 노벨상
  • 멀티모달 (Multimodal)
    GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 등 텍스트, 음성, 영상을 실시간 처리하는 모델 경쟁.
  • 노벨상 수상
    존 홉필드, 제프리 힌튼(노벨 물리학상) 및 데미스 하사비스(노벨 화학상) 수상으로 AI의 과학적 공로 인정.
2025: 생각하고(Reasoning), 행동하며(Agent), 몸을 가진(Physical) 지능 연구
  • 추론 모델 (Reasoning Models)
    Google Gemini, OpenAI o1/o3 등 '생각의 사슬(Chain of Thought)'을 통해 복잡한 문제를 해결하는 AI의 확산.
  • 에이전트 AI (Agentic AI)
    AI가 도구를 사용하고 스스로 계획을 세우는 '에이전트'의 상용화가 시작되었으나, 아직 부족함.
  • 물리적 지능(Physical AI)
    언어 모델을 넘어 로봇 공학(Humanoid)과 결합하려는 시도의 본격화.
  • 월드 모델 (World Models)
    물리 법칙과 공간의 인과관계를 시뮬레이션하려는 연구 심화.
  • 재귀적 자기 개선 (Recursive Self-Improvement)
